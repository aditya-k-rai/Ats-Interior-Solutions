To convert affluent homeowners looking to spend ₹10 Lakhs+ on modular kitchens and ₹20 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore+ on turnkey interior architecture, your website cannot look like a standard contractor or mass-market aggregator site.
In luxury web psychology, restraint equals prestige. Mass-market websites use vibrant, saturated colors (bright blue, orange, red) to scream for attention. Luxury websites use deeply muted, architectural palettes that act as a quiet art gallery—letting your portfolio photos and craftsmanship take center stage.

Gradient Science: Are Gradients Good for Luxury Websites?
Yes, but ONLY if they mimic physical light or brushed materials.
What to Avoid:
Never use multi-color neon, purple-to-orange, or web3/tech gradients. In the luxury home space, these instantly look artificial, trendy, and low-cost.
What to Use:
Affluent clients respond to micro-gradients that simulate natural architectural lighting, subtle depth, and brushed metals.
1. The "Brushed Champagne Metallic" Gradient (For CTAs, Form Submit Buttons, & Badges)
This gradient replicates the soft reflection of brushed brass and champagne aluminum trims on German kitchen cabinets.
Type: Linear Gradient at 135 degrees
Color Stops:
0%: #D8C29D (Luminous Highlight)
50%: #C5A880 (Champagne Body)
100%: #A88452 (Deep Satin Shadow)
Usage: Use on your main call-to-action button ("Schedule VIP Design Consultation").
2. The "Architectural Spotlight" Radial Gradient (For Hero Sections & Dark Backgrounds)
Mimics high-end track spotlighting illuminating a dark gallery wall.
Type: Radial Gradient (Center-Outward)
Center (0%): #1E232B (Soft Warm Dark Tint)
Edge (100%): #0B0D10 (Deep Shadow Obsidian)
Usage: Use as the background for hero banners and client testimonial cards.
3. The "Soft Mist / Frosted Travertine" Gradient (For Light Mode Feature Cards)
Creates a subtle tactile feel for specification cards (e.g., German Blum Hardware, Hafele Fittings, Quartz Countertops).
Type: Linear Gradient at 180 degrees (Top to Bottom)
Top (0%): #FFFFFF (Pure Light)
Bottom (100%): #F0ECE3 (Warm Travertine Stone)
Visual Hierarchy & Conversion Framework (The 60-30-10 Rule)
To maintain maximum conversion impact and avoid overwhelming the client, structure your color distribution across the layout:
60% (The Canvas): Keep backgrounds clean (either Alabaster #FBFBF9 or Deep Slate #0D0F12). This ensures your high-resolution photographs of 4 BHK living rooms and island kitchens look vivid and uncompressed.
30% (The Structure): Use deep charcoals, greiges, and rich woods for typography, navigation bars, and category headers to create visual balance.
10% (The Conversion Trigger): Reserve your Champagne Gold/Brass accent strictly for interactive elements:
Primary Action Buttons ("Book Design Consultation", "Download Portfolio")
Key qualification indicators ("Projects starting from ₹20 Lakhs")
Highlight icons (e.g., "10-Year Warranty", "100% Factory Finish")
